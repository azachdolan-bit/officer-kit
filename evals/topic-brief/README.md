# topic-brief tests

Every file here is fictional and exists only for the harness (`evals/topic_brief_check.py`, 31 items): `source.md` is made up after action notes on a night fight at a made up hill, `doctrine.md` a made up publication. The good brief, script, and card pass; the bad ones fail on each planted defect (an opening that misses the topic, a two paragraph context, an untraced number, a forbidden line, a dash; a script over time with a mark out of place, a quote no source carries, and a doctrine named but not quoted; a card with a sentence for key words, marks out of order, an uncited quote, and a stray mark).

## Blind test (27 Sep 26)

A drafter with only the skill, one research file on one lieutenant's night in a real battle, and the library's copy of the doctrinal passage was given a real assignment: a five minute history presentation, one minute in, three in the body, one out, with a specific doctrinal element. None of it is included here.

1. **Topic first.** The brief opened on the topic and kept context to one paragraph of 99 words. With sources narrower than the topic, the drafter scoped to what they supported and named the rest as a gap; the skill now says to do exactly that.
2. **Traps avoided.** The research file carried four: a casualty figure for two days and two regimental combat teams, a disputed name for the attack's leader, a conversation remembered decades later, and a range of enemy counts. The script said none of them wrongly, and all four went into lines not to say.
3. **Doctrine shown, not named.** The script quoted the passage and applied it; the checker verified the words against the library copy.
4. **Checkers.** The script ran 4:41 at 140 words a minute with every mark within 20 seconds. The drafter's first card passed its checks but lost a line on the page because three quotations did not fit one face; the builder now counts a quotation's wrapped lines.
5. **Evidence gate.** The blind reviewer graded 37 claims: 20 substantive, 11 thin, 6 reaching, none wrong, and every number matched its source. What it caught was scope: a regimental combat team's figure said of the regiments, one battalion's experience said of every Marine, a compiler's heading taken for the source. Those are now rules in the brief shape, and `--numbers` lists every number beside its sentence for that read.
6. **Visual gate.** NO SHIP on the first pass: a diary's clock time written in m:ss form read as a pacing cue, citations were about 7 point, and one face had no time mark. The builder now fails the first and last and sets citations at 9 point.

## Against the user's own script

The checker was run on the author's machine against a script the author had already delivered (not included here). Every number traced to its sources table, every time mark fell within 20 seconds of where the words put it, the format split held, the doctrinal quote verified against the library copy, and no forbidden line was spoken. It failed only on the header lines this tool's layout adds, and on one quotation whose source had not been passed to it.
