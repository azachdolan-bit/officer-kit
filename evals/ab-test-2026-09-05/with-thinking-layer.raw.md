## Phases

1. Movement: loading in the company area before first light and vehicle movement to Range 7.
2. Setup: ammunition draw and staging, range setup, safety and communications checks.
3. Execution day one: Table 2 serials, including barricade and movement drills, transitions between firing positions, and malfunction handling.
4. Execution day two: the same serials, in the afternoon heat, with a tired company.
5. Recovery: clearing, police call, ammunition turn in, range closeout.
6. Return: movement back to the company area at the end of day two.

## Highest residual level: ID

Hazards 2 and 5 both hold at severity I, probability D, which is HIGH on Figure 3-4. Six other hazards sit at MEDIUM and six at LOW.

## High risk training: no  (yes if any residual is IA, IB, IIA, IIB)

No residual level is IA, IB, IIA or IIB, so this event is not High Risk Training under paragraph 040302, and Table 2 marksmanship is not among the examples at 040303. Read the next line before treating that as good news: two hazards keep a residual severity of I, meaning the worst credible outcome is still a death, and the commanding officer may elect to treat the event as high risk anyway. The four elements the order requires of a high risk worksheet are written below whether or not that election is made, because they are what the OIC and the RSO will actually use on the day.

## Approval authority: battalion commanding officer, the first O-5 in the chain

## Emergency action plan

Immediate actions, in sequence. Every bracket is confirmed with range control during the leaders reconnaissance and entered before the sheet is routed.

1. Any Marine calls CEASE FIRE. The RSO repeats it, the tower repeats it, and every weapon goes to Condition 4 in place under a safety.
2. The safety nearest the casualty renders care and holds the scene. Nobody else moves toward it.
3. The corpsman moves to the casualty with the trauma kit from the entry control point. The RSO sends the evacuation vehicle to the lane.
4. The RSO reports to range control on the primary net: location by lane, number of casualties, nature, and evacuation intent. Call signs and net: [enter from the range control frequency assignment].
5. The OIC calls [range control telephone: enter] and [the installation emergency number: enter]. Battalion is notified after the casualty is moving, not before.
6. Ground evacuation departs by the route driven during the leaders reconnaissance to [receiving facility: enter], estimated [minutes: enter]. Air evacuation is requested through range control; the landing point is [enter], marked by the platoon sergeant.
7. Equipment shutdown: all firing stops, the range goes cold, weapons are cleared under a safety, ammunition is returned to the staging tarp, and vehicle engines are shut down except the evacuation vehicle.
8. Muster and control of the scene: all unaffected Marines muster by squad at the entry control point; squad leaders report the count to the RSO; the position of the weapon, the brass and the shooter is left untouched until range control releases the scene.
9. The OIC records who was where, in writing, before anyone leaves the range.

The plan is walked through on the ground during setup on day one, before the first relay goes hot. When this event is designated high risk, the order requires the unit high risk training safety officer to validate this plan quarterly by a walk through.

## Cease training and training time out

Any Marine, of any rank, and any observer stops the event by calling CEASE FIRE for a live fire condition or TRAINING TIME OUT for anything else, by voice and by both arms raised and crossed overhead. The tower repeats the call and the range goes cold.

Nobody restarts the event but the RSO, and only after the condition that caused the call is fixed, not explained. The RSO tells the OIC what was called, by whom, and what changed before firing resumed. No Marine is questioned or corrected for making the call, including a call that turns out to be unnecessary.

## Communications

Primary: the range control net on [frequency and call signs: enter], with the tower manned whenever a relay is hot.
Secondary: cell coverage at the tower is confirmed during setup on day one; if it fails, the runner and the evacuation vehicle radio are the alternate, and the RSO says so at the safety brief.
Check: radio check with range control before the first relay each day, at every serial change, and at the close of each day. A failed check stops firing until it is restored.

Confirmed the morning of each day by the RSO and reported to the OIC.

- [ ] Head count against the roster, and every change from the count on this sheet identified by squad.
- [ ] Corpsman present, trauma kit and litter staged at the entry control point, certification current.
- [ ] Evacuation vehicle staged with its driver present, route confirmed with range control today.
- [ ] Radio check with range control complete on the primary net; secondary means confirmed.
- [ ] Flag condition read and posted; work rest cycle set for that flag; both water points filled and positioned.
- [ ] Left and right limit stakes emplaced and confirmed from the tower.
- [ ] Safeties posted at one per two firing points, briefed on their lane and on the stop trigger.
- [ ] Double hearing protection and eye protection inspected on every Marine going to the line.
- [ ] Ammunition count on hand against the issue document.
- [ ] Anything that changed since this sheet was signed is stated aloud to the OIC, who decides whether it changes a control.

## Supervision

| Control | Who watches it | Stop trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Flag condition check and work rest cycle | RSO, hourly, recorded on the range log | Flag rises a category, or any Marine shows heat symptoms |
| Water points staffed and drinking at each rest cycle | Platoon sergeants at the ready line | A water point runs dry, or a squad passes a rest cycle without drinking |
| Condition 4 confirmed at every transition | Line safeties, each transition | Any weapon leaves a position not in Condition 4 |
| Limit stakes and muzzle discipline | RSO from the tower, safeties on the line | A muzzle passes a limit stake, or a stake is knocked down |
| Malfunction handling by the safety, not the shooter | Line safeties, continuous | A shooter clears a malfunction unsupervised |
| Hearing and eye protection worn | Squad leaders at the ready line | Any shooter reaches the line without both |
| Ground guides and speed on the access road | Motor transport chief, each movement | A vehicle moves without its guide, or exceeds 15 miles an hour on the unimproved section |
| Evacuation vehicle and driver posted | RSO, every live serial | The vehicle or the driver leaves the entry control point |
| Operator rest before the return movement | Squad leaders, end of day two | Any operator has not slept, or no second operator is available |
