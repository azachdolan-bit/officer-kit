# RED TEAM: two Risk Assessment Worksheets, company live fire Table 2 at Range 7, against MCO 5100.29C Volume 2 (extracts) and the annotated exemplar

Four passes were run on each worksheet independently, in the order below, with the premortem written before either draft was read closely. Nothing is scored. Findings are ranked by consequence inside each pass.

What the tasking supplies, in full, because every "invented fact" finding is measured against this list: a company live fire range next month; Table 2 at Range 7; two days; the whole company; the event was run last year and "went fine"; one Marine "got dehydrated on the second day"; a corpsman will be present; the requester is the OIC; the requester's platoon sergeant is the RSO; battalion wants it by Friday for signature. Nothing else.

# PART ONE — WORKSHEET 1

## Premortem

Asserted failure: a Marine was hurt on this two-day company live fire and this worksheet is now enclosure (1) to the command investigation. The investigating officer is reading it to find out whether the hazard that hurt him was on the sheet, and whether the control that was supposed to stop it existed on paper only.

Reasons written before reading the draft, then checked against it:

1. **The sheet has no hazard for the phase where people actually get hurt on a live fire range — the weapon in the hand of a Marine who is moving.** (Handled.) Hazard 6, negligent discharge during the transition between firing positions, severity I, held at severity I after controls, residual ID. This is the sheet's highest residual and it is the right one to be highest.

2. **The sheet treats last year's dehydration case as the whole medical picture and prices heat as the main threat, so the gunshot wound has no medical plan behind it.** (Partly handled.) Hazard 13 carries evacuation delay at severity I with a corpsman, a staged vehicle, a marked landing zone and a comms check. What is absent is any tourniquet, IFAK or combat-lifesaver capability at the firing point itself: the sheet's severity I mechanisms are penetrating trauma, and the nearest intervention it names is a corpsman "positioned at the line with a trauma bag." One provider, one bag, for a company on two days.

3. **Every control is executed by an RSO who cannot be in four places at once, and nothing in the sheet notices that.** (Not handled.) The RSO is the named actor in the controls for hazards 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 11 and 13, in eight of eleven supervision rows, and in EAP steps 1 and 3. He is simultaneously required to confirm lateral limits before each relay, check the wet bulb hourly, check the fire condition hourly, walk each lane, walk the line with every safety, and hold cease fire authority on the primary net — for approximately 180 Marines across two days, with no assistant RSO, no relief, and no hazard for his own degradation. The sheet has a hazard for line safety complacency on day two (hazard 11) and none for the single person on whom every one of those controls depends.

4. **The RSO is the OIC's own platoon sergeant, so the man with stop authority works for the man running the event, and the sheet never says he can stop him.** (Partly handled.) The cease training paragraph says "Any Marine on the range, of any rank, and any observer, may stop the event" and "Nobody is questioned or corrected for calling one," which reaches the problem generically. The specific structural fact — that the two key safety billets come from the same platoon and the same daily chain of command — is in the tasking and is not addressed anywhere on the sheet.

5. **The event runs two days and the sheet plans one.** (Not handled.) Six phases are listed; phases 1 through 5 run from movement out to recovery, and phase 6 is "Movement back." There is no phase, no hazard, and no control for the night between day one and day two: what happens to ammunition, weapons accountability, and personnel between the end of firing on day one and the start of firing on day two is not on the sheet. Whether the company returns to the company area each night or stays out is never stated, and either answer generates hazards the sheet does not carry.

6. **A control is written that reads well and cannot be executed, and the investigation finds it was never done.** (Partly handled.) Most controls have a who and a when in the exemplar's manner. Two do not survive contact: "the published number of relays" (hazard 11) refers to a document that is not part of the sheet, and "the range day to ten hours" is a number the sheet invents and the range schedule may not permit.

## Expected but absent

- **A hazard for the return movement, on a sheet that lists the return movement as a phase.** Phase 6 exists in the phase list and has no line in the hazard table. A company driving home at the end of a two-day range day is the classic mishap, and the sheet's own structure advertises the hole: an investigating officer counting phases against hazards finds it in ten seconds. This is the single most visible gap in the draft.
- **A hazard for weapon malfunction and hot chamber clearing.** Nowhere in the sheet. On a live fire range the malfunction is the moment when an untrained Marine points a loaded weapon at himself or turns it off the marked direction, and the sheet's phase 3 does not even list malfunction handling among the day's activities.
- **A hazard for hot brass driving an uncontrolled weapon movement.** The sheet has brass at hazard 9, rated as an eye injury at severity III. The consequential version of hot brass on a live line is not the eye, it is the Marine who breaks position with a loaded rifle.
- **Lightning, severe weather, or any range closure criterion.** The only environmental hazard is heat (7) and fire (4). Two days on an outdoor range in October with no weather stop criterion and no statement of who calls it.
- **The prior year's worksheet.** Paragraph 040304.B is explicit that a repeated event's worksheet is reused "once it is updated with current information, and all risk assessments and assumptions are carefully reviewed and verified." The tasking says the event was run last year. The sheet nowhere asks for last year's worksheet or states that none exists — the first document a good version of this product requests.
- **The root cause of last year's dehydration case.** The sheet uses that case to set hazard 7's probability (gap 6) and never asks why it happened. If the cause was that the water at the line ran out, then "two water sources staged at the line" is precisely the control that failed last year, and the sheet is recycling it without knowing that.
- **Any qualification standard for the line safeties.** Line safeties appear in the controls for hazards 2, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 12 and in four supervision rows. Nothing in the sheet says who they are, that they are trained, or that they are certified. The exemplar's discipline is that a control has a who; "a line safety" is a role, not a who, when nothing establishes the role is filled by someone qualified.
- **A combination-of-hazards statement.** Paragraph 030302.C: "the combination of two or more hazards may increase the overall level of risk." Day two on this sheet stacks heat (7), safety complacency (11), fatigue, and a loaded line (6). The sheet rates each separately and never aggregates. Compare the exemplar, which also does not aggregate — so this is a finding against the standard rather than against the exemplar.
- **Against the exemplar specifically:** the exemplar's approval line is arithmetic and this draft's is too, and the exemplar's discipline that "probability moved; severity did not" is held on all thirteen lines. The exemplar covers movement, setup, serials, night and recovery; this draft covers movement, setup, two execution days and recovery, and drops the return. The exemplar's difference that matters is that its eight hazards each name a mechanism, a phase and a condition — this draft's thirteen do too, which is why the missing lines are the finding and the written lines are not.

## Adversarial read

- **"No night fire is assumed. If any relay runs after end of evening nautical twilight, the sheet needs illumination, night limit marking, and driving hazards added before it is signed."** The condition attaches to signature, not to execution. Once the O-5 signs on Friday, this sentence is spent: a day two that slips two hours and finishes the last relay in the dark complies with the sheet as written, because the sheet was signed before the slip. Rewrite: "If any relay would run after end of evening nautical twilight, firing stops at twilight; adding night relays requires illumination, night limit marking and driving hazards on this sheet and a new signature."
- **"Firing schedule limits each Marine to the published number of relays and the range day to ten hours, held by the OIC."** "The published number" is unbounded — a schedule that publishes twelve relays complies with the control. The control constrains nothing it does not already assume. Rewrite: name the number on this sheet, or state the limit as a maximum relays per Marine per day.
- **"one line safety positioned behind every four firers"** sets a ratio and no floor. With relays sized to the range, the sheet complies with a total safety count that may be a handful of Marines for a company of 180, and complies whether or not those Marines have ever stood a line before. Rewrite: state the total number of safeties required to run the event and that each is qualified by name on the roster before the first relay.
- **"RSO checks the wet bulb reading hourly and applies the matching work and rest cycle"** — read for the inconvenient compliant meaning, the RSO reads and applies, and if the resulting work/rest cycle cannot be reconciled with the relay schedule, the sheet says nothing about which yields. The ten-hour day is the only competing constraint and it points the other way. Rewrite: state that the work/rest cycle governs the schedule, and that relays are cut, not compressed, when the two conflict.
- **"extinguishers and one water source staged at the firing line by the company gunnery sergeant"** (hazard 4) alongside **"two water sources staged at the line"** (hazard 7). "One water source" for firefighting is ambiguous as to whether it is one of the two drinking sources. If it is, the fire control adds nothing and both controls are degraded at once. Rewrite: name the firefighting water separately from the drinking water.
- **"the mechanism that put one Marine down on day two last year"** (hazard 7) reads two ways: that the Marine collapsed, or that he was pulled off the line. The tasking says he "got dehydrated." The reading is the finding — the sheet's probability rests on how bad last year's case was, and the sheet does not know.
- **"Delay in casualty evacuation from the firing line to definitive care at the distance Range 7 sits from the nearest treatment facility"** (hazard 13) states a distance-driven hazard without a distance or a time. A count with no denominator: the residual IE is a claim that the delay has been reduced, and there is no number on either side of it. Rewrite: enter the ground evacuation time from the line to the receiving facility and state the threshold above which the event does not run.
- **Acceptable inconvenient readings, examined and dismissed:** "magazines stay empty until the RSO clears the relay onto the line" (hazard 3) permits loading at the line, which is the intent; "the posted convoy speed" (hazard 1) defers to an authority outside the sheet, which is correct for a speed limit; the pre-execution item "If a change adds a hazard the sheet does not carry, it gets added and the sheet goes back for signature" is the strongest sentence in the draft and closes the loop the first bullet above leaves open.

## Against the standard

- **040304.B: "There is no required form for the risk assessment worksheet, but it should include the following: 1. An Emergency Action Plan..."** (met). All twelve named minimum elements appear: primary and alternate communications (EAP 6, Communications), telephone numbers (EAP 5, four categories, as placeholders), radio channels and call signs (EAP 6, as placeholders), locations of emergency response personnel and equipment (EAP 7), equipment shutdown procedures (EAP 8), muster site and control of the scene (EAP 9), non-affected personnel (EAP 10), and immediate procedures in sequence (EAP 1-4, 11-12). The form is a sequential list of responses, which is what the paragraph asks for.
- **040304.B.1: "Individual EAPs shall be validated quarterly by a walk-through from the unit HRTSO."** (met) — "This plan is validated by a walk through on the range the day prior, and quarterly thereafter by the unit high risk training safety officer for as long as the event repeats."
- **040304.B.2: "Procedures shall be outlined WRT personnel/student Cease Training (CT)/Training Time Out (TTO) requirements, and training will not reconvene until the identified issue is resolved."** (met) — the cease training paragraph names who may call it, the words, three signals, the actions on call, and states "Training does not reconvene until the issue is resolved."
- **040304.B.3: "A primary and secondary communications plan for stopping training if anyone observes an unsafe condition."** (met) — primary range control net, secondary battalion tactical net, cell phone third, with a stated failure rule ("A failed check on both means the range is cold until it is restored").
- **040304.B.4: "A pre-execution final brief checklist to confirm any changes are accounted for."** (met) — eleven items, and the change item routes a new hazard back for signature.
- **040302, high risk training:** the residual pair test is applied correctly — no residual sits at IA, IB, IIA or IIB, so the event is not HRT by the second sentence of 040302, and the arithmetic checks against Figure 3-4 on all thirteen lines. **Partly met on the first sentence.** 040302 also defines HRT as "training which exposes personnel and trainers to the risk of death, serious injury, or permanent disability despite the presence of proper safety controls." This sheet retains three residuals at severity I (hazards 5, 6, 13) — that is, risk of death after controls. The sheet applies the pair test and does not put the definitional sentence in front of the signer. The determination is not wrong; the tension is undisclosed, and the disclosure belongs to the officer who decides.
- **040304.A / risk decision authority:** approval routed to the battalion commander (O-5) because hazard 6 holds at ID, HIGH on Figure 3-4, per the kit's default table for "any other EH or H." Correct, and the sheet states that a published battalion table governs instead. (met)
- **030302, severity and probability:** severity is held constant on every one of the thirteen lines — no line drops severity, which is the exemplar's stated discipline and the weak exemplar's stated defect. Three lines move probability two steps (hazards 3, 4, 12), all at severity II or III; none of the severity I lines moves more than one step. **Departure at hazard 9:** "Eye injury from ejected brass or a ricochet fragment" is rated severity III, "Minor injury or illness." Figure 3-2 Category II is "Permanent partial disability or severe injury or illness." Loss of an eye is permanent partial disability. The rating understates the hazard as the sheet itself words it. Defect, not local practice.
- **030302.D pitfalls, "Over-optimism, not being totally honest or not looking for root causes."** (partly met) The sheet resists the tasking's "it went fine" to the extent of gap 6 — "If there were other near misses last year, they belong on this sheet" — and does not look for the root cause of the one case it has.
- **030302.C, "the combination of two or more hazards may increase the overall level of risk."** (not met) No aggregation anywhere; each of thirteen hazards is rated alone.
- **040304.B, reuse: "For regularly repeated training events the same worksheet can be used once it is updated with current information, and all risk assessments and assumptions are carefully reviewed and verified."** (not met) The event was run last year. The sheet does not call for the prior worksheet or record that none was found.

1. **No hazard for the weapon in the hand of a moving Marine.** (Handled.) Two severity I mechanisms on the firing line, both held at severity I after controls. Correct.

2. **Heat priced as the main threat, no medical plan behind the gunshot wound.** (Partly handled.) What is absent, as in the other draft, is any tourniquet or combat lifesaver capability at the firing point; the whole medical answer is one corpsman and a trauma kit at the entry control point, and the sheet's own Notes concede that two residual levels rest on that corpsman.

## Expected but absent

- **A range fire hazard, and any fire response at all.** The sheet has no fire hazard, no extinguisher anywhere in fourteen hazards, eleven checklist items and nine supervision rows, and no fire step in the emergency action plan. A live fire range in October with tracer, hot brass and impact, and the EAP has no answer to a fire on the range. The EAP's equipment shutdown step covers vehicles and weapons and not a fire.
- **A hazard for the degradation of the safeties on day two.** Phase 4 is described as "the same serials, in the afternoon heat, with a tired company" — the sheet identifies fatigue as the day two condition and then rates the company's ankles (11), the company's ears (10), the company's heat exposure (1) and the drivers' fatigue (14). The line safeties, who execute the controls for hazards 2, 3, 4, 5, 9 and 11, get no fatigue hazard, and they are the layer the severity I residuals rest on.
- **A hazard for a live round leaving the range.** The sheet has the dud on the ground (13) and count reconciliation at turn-in, and nothing for the round in a cargo pocket, a magazine or a vehicle bed. The count catches what is issued against what returns; it does not catch the round in a pocket if the count reconciles.
- **Any statement of whether firing occurs at night.** The sheet asserts darkness twice — "before first light," "in the dark" — for the movement, and never says whether any relay is fired after dark, which would drive illumination, night limit marking and a different control set entirely.
- **Lightning, severe weather, or a range closure criterion.** Heat is the only environmental hazard.
- **The prior year's worksheet.** 040304.B makes it the first document to ask for on a repeated event. The sheet recites last year's outcome in its header and never asks for the sheet that governed it.
- **The root cause of last year's dehydration case.** Used to set hazard 1's probability at B; never interrogated.
- **Any qualification standard for line safeties.** They appear in eight controls and the sheet checks the corpsman's certification in the pre-execution checklist and not theirs.
- **A combination-of-hazards statement per 030302.C.** The sheet comes closer than most — "two hazards keep a residual severity of I, meaning the worst credible outcome is still a death" — but that is a statement about two hazards separately, not an aggregation.
- **Against the exemplar specifically:** the exemplar's approval line is arithmetic and this draft's is; the exemplar's rule that probability moves and severity does not is held on all fourteen lines. The exemplar's "Assumptions" discipline is where the difference bites — the exemplar's annotation is that "the sheet does not pretend" its controls did more than they did, and this draft's Notes for the approver make a completeness claim the sheet does not support (see the adversarial read and the invented-fact list below).

## Adversarial read

- **"Battalion is notified after the casualty is moving, not before."** This is a directive to delay reporting to higher headquarters, and it is unbounded. Read for the inconvenient compliant meaning: a fatality, or a casualty who cannot be moved for forty minutes because extraction from the lane is complicated, means battalion learns nothing for forty minutes, and the sheet requires that. It also conflicts with the same sheet's requirement that the OIC not restart without anyone above him. Rewrite: "The OIC notifies the battalion COC as soon as the nine-line has passed to range control, and in any case within ten minutes of the cease fire, whether or not the casualty is moving."
- **"Nobody restarts the event but the RSO, and only after the condition that caused the call is fixed, not explained."** The first clause vests restart in the RSO exclusively — the OIC, who owns the event and the risk decision, is excluded by the word "nobody." The next sentence, "The RSO tells the OIC what was called, by whom, and what changed before firing resumed," is ambiguous as to sequence: "before firing resumed" may qualify what changed rather than when the telling happens, so the sheet permits the RSO to restart the range and inform the OIC afterwards. Rewrite: "The RSO reports to the OIC what was called, by whom, and what was fixed; the OIC authorizes the restart."
- **"safeties position no shooter closer to the barricade than the range regulations allow."** The control is a pointer to a document the sheet does not name, cite, or attach. Any distance complies if nobody on the line knows the number. A standard cited with no paragraph. Rewrite: enter the minimum distance in feet on this sheet, taken from the Range 7 range packet at the leaders reconnaissance.
- **"the motor transport chief limits the return movement to daylight where the schedule allows."** The qualifier destroys the control. The schedule allows daylight return on the day nothing goes wrong, and does not allow it on the day the range runs long — which is the only day the control matters. Rewrite: "The return movement departs no later than the time that puts the last vehicle in the company area before end of evening nautical twilight; if firing would push past that time, firing stops."
- **"the OIC holds day two firing to before 1400 when the flag is black."** Two inconvenient readings, both compliant. The limit applies only when the flag is black, so a red flag afternoon on day two is unconstrained; and it applies only to day two, so an identical black flag afternoon on day one is unconstrained. Rewrite: state the work/rest and firing limits by flag condition, applying to both days.
- **"the safety clears every malfunction with the muzzle downrange and holds a hot chamber closed for 10 seconds before opening it."** A specific procedural interval with no authority behind it. Where the installation range regulation or the weapon's technical manual specifies a longer cook-off wait, this sheet directs a shorter one and the safety who follows the sheet is wrong. Rewrite: cite the interval to the range regulation or the technical manual, or state it as "not less than" the local requirement.
- **"cell coverage at the tower is confirmed during setup on day one; if it fails, the runner and the evacuation vehicle radio are the alternate."** The alternate communications means is the radio in the vehicle the EAP dispatches off the range with the casualty (EAP 3 and 6). At the moment communications matter most, the alternate has left. Rewrite: name a third means that stays on the range.
- **"Each missing item is a gap rather than an omission, and each one is bracketed above."** This is a completeness claim made to the approving officer, and it is not true of the sheet it describes: at least twenty-five facts the tasking does not supply are asserted flat, unbracketed, and are listed below. This is the sentence that does the most damage in front of an investigating officer, because it tells the O-5 that what he cannot see is nothing.
- **"The corpsman's presence is the one assumption this sheet cannot carry on its own."** "The one" is a second completeness claim in the same paragraph, and the same list refutes it. Rewrite both: "The bracketed items are the known gaps. Additional assumptions carried in the hazard table are listed at [x] and each requires confirmation before signature."
- **"squad leaders check that each operator slept during the night between the two days."** Unverifiable as a control, and it silently asserts a night on the range that no phase in the sheet covers. Rewrite: state the overnight plan as a phase, and control operator rest by scheduling crew rest, not by asking a squad leader to certify sleep.
- **"Six other hazards sit at MEDIUM and six at LOW."** Checkable and wrong. Against Figure 3-4 the sheet's own fourteen residuals are two HIGH, seven MEDIUM, five LOW. Rewrite: seven MEDIUM and five LOW.
- **Acceptable inconvenient readings, examined and dismissed:** "the lead vehicle holds 15 miles an hour on the unimproved section" is a hard number and complies only one way; "the RSO holds spare plugs at the entry control point and stops the relay until every shooter is fitted" has a stop trigger inside the control; "only after the condition that caused the call is fixed, not explained" is the strongest phrase in either draft's cease training paragraph.

## Against the standard

- **040304.B.1, the EAP minimum content.** (partly met.) Present: primary and alternate communications (EAP 4, Communications), radio channels and call signs (EAP 4, bracketed), locations of emergency response personnel and equipment (EAP 3 and 6, partial), equipment shutdown procedures (EAP 7), muster site and control of the scene (EAP 8), non-affected personnel (EAP 8), and immediate procedures in sequence (EAP 1-9). **Not met on telephone numbers:** the paragraph requires "telephone numbers"; the EAP carries two, range control and the installation emergency number, and has no number for the receiving facility or for the battalion COC, which is the headquarters the sheet elsewhere requires the OIC to notify. **Partly met on locations of emergency equipment:** there is no fire extinguisher anywhere on the sheet, so the EAP's equipment inventory is incomplete against the hazards a live fire range presents.
- **040304.B.1, "Individual EAPs shall be validated quarterly by a walk-through from the unit HRTSO."** (partly met, and the departure is deliberate.) The sheet says: "When this event is designated high risk, the order requires the unit high risk training safety officer to validate this plan quarterly by a walk through." The sheet has already concluded the event is not high risk, so the effect of its own sentence is that the quarterly walk-through never happens. The reading is defensible — the requirement sits inside 040304.B, which opens "High Risk Training events should use a risk assessment worksheet" — but it is a reading that removes a validation the sheet would otherwise get, and it should be recorded as a deliberate local position in the override file rather than left as a conditional sentence on the sheet. The sheet does provide a substitute: "walked through on the ground during setup on day one, before the first relay goes hot." That substitute is weaker than a walk-through the day prior, because a defect found during setup on day one has no time to be fixed.
- **040304.B.2, CT/TTO.** (met) Who may call it, both words, the signal, the tower's repeat, the range going cold, the restart condition, and the protection of the caller.
- **040304.B.3, primary and secondary communications for stopping training.** (met) Primary range control net, secondary cell, alternate runner and vehicle radio, with a stated failure rule. Subject to the circularity finding above.

## 2. Where the two disagree on a rating for substantially the same hazard

- **Heat casualty. Worksheet 1: IIC initial. Worksheet 2: IIB initial.** Both land on IID residual, so nothing downstream changes. **The standard supports C.** Figure 3-3 defines B as "Expected to occur frequently to an individual item or person" and C as "Expected to occur several times to an individual item or person" — the scale is written per individual. One case, in one prior iteration, across a whole company over two days, is not frequent occurrence to an individual. Worksheet 2's B leans toward the pitfall the order names at 030302.D as "Alarmism, worst case scenarios are used regardless of their possibility."
- **Hearing loss. Worksheet 1: "Permanent hearing loss," severity II, IIC to IID (residual MEDIUM). Worksheet 2: "Hearing loss," severity III, IIIB to IIID (residual LOW).** **The standard supports II.** Figure 3-2 Category II is "Permanent partial disability or severe injury or illness"; Category III is "Minor injury or illness." Neither sheet qualifies the loss as temporary, and noise-induced hearing loss on a live fire line is permanent. The consequence is real, not cosmetic: worksheet 2's rating puts hearing at LOW, which is the tier that gets watched least.
- **Ricochet and ejected brass injury. Worksheet 1: "Eye injury from ejected brass or a ricochet fragment," severity III, IIID to IIIE. Worksheet 2: "Ricochet or fragment," severity II, IIC to IID.** **The standard supports II.** Loss of an eye is permanent partial disability, Category II. Worksheet 1 names the eye in its own hazard text and then rates the injury as minor.
- **Ammunition at the draw and staging point. Worksheet 1: severity II, IIC to IIE. Worksheet 2: severity III, IIIC to IIIE.** **The standard supports II for the hazard as worksheet 2 words it,** because worksheet 2's text includes "or lost," and a lost round is not "minor damage to equipment, systems, property, or the environment." For dropped or mixed rounds alone, III is supported. Worksheet 2 should either raise the severity or remove the word.
- **Evacuation delay. Worksheet 1: ID initial. Worksheet 2: IC initial.** Both reach IE residual. **Both are supportable, for different reasons, and the difference is one of method rather than of rating.** Worksheet 2 defines the hazard by the absence of its own controls — "delayed because the ground route from Range 7 was never driven and the corpsman was at the tower" — and against that pre-control state, C is right. But a hazard defined by its controls being absent guarantees the reduction that follows, and worksheet 2 then moves probability two steps, C to E, on its most consequential severity, resting on an assumption its own Notes say the sheet cannot carry. Worksheet 1 states the hazard as delay from distance alone, for which D is the supported reading. If both were worded the same way, D.

Where they agree, they agree exactly and it is worth the signer knowing: negligent discharge at transition (IC to ID), round outside the limits (ID to IE), vehicle rollover (ID to IE), and ankle or knee injury (IIIC to IIID). Both sheets compute a highest residual of ID, both find the event is not high risk training under the 040302 pair test, and both route to the battalion commander as the first O-5. Two independently produced sheets landing on the same top line and the same approval authority is the strongest single piece of evidence in this package that the routing is right.

One count fails a mechanical check. Worksheet 2 states "Six other hazards sit at MEDIUM and six at LOW." Against Figure 3-4, its own fourteen residuals are two HIGH, **seven** MEDIUM and **five** LOW. Worksheet 1 publishes no distribution and so has no equivalent claim to check. Every other level in both sheets computes correctly, and neither sheet drops a severity anywhere.
