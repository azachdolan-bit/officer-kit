## Emergency action plan

Order of actions, in sequence, by the person in control of the event.

1. Whoever sees it calls CEASE FIRE. RSO repeats it on the primary net and by whistle. All weapons to Condition 4, all firers stand fast in place.
2. Corpsman moves to the casualty from the firing line position. Nobody else moves toward the line.
3. RSO reports to the OIC: what happened, where on the range, how many, condition.
4. OIC calls range control on the primary net with a nine line, then the battalion COC on the secondary.
5. Telephone numbers, posted at the exit control point and carried by the OIC and the RSO: range control <number, confirm at the range safety brief>; base emergency services 911 from a cell, <number> from a landline; nearest treatment facility <number>; battalion COC <number>. Confirm every number on the range safety walk the day prior and again at the pre execution check.
6. Radio channels and call signs: primary <range control net, channel and call signs from the range packet>; secondary <battalion tactical net>. Both posted at the exit control point.
7. Locations of emergency response personnel and equipment: corpsman and trauma bag at the firing line; litter, cooling gear, and spare water at the exit control point; evacuation vehicle at the exit control point, staged nose out, driver present; extinguishers at the firing line and at the ammunition point.
8. Equipment shutdown: all firing stops at CEASE FIRE, magazines out, chambers clear, weapons grounded muzzle down range. Vehicles other than the evacuation vehicle shut down and stay in the staging area.
9. Muster site and control of the scene: staging area at the exit control point. Company gunnery sergeant musters by relay and reports a head count to the OIC. RSO controls the line and keeps everyone off it. Nothing on the line moves until the OIC releases it.
10. Non affected personnel: platoon commanders hold their platoons at the muster site, seated, weapons Condition 4, until the OIC releases the range.
11. Evacuation: ground evacuation by the staged vehicle to the nearest treatment facility, route reconnoitered by the OIC and the driver the day prior. Landing zone at <grid, from the range packet> if range control launches air.
12. OIC reports the mishap to the battalion COC and does not restart the event without the battalion commander's release.

This plan is validated by a walk through on the range the day prior, and quarterly thereafter by the unit high risk training safety officer for as long as the event repeats.

## Cease training and training time out

Any Marine on the range, of any rank, and any observer, may stop the event. The word is CEASE FIRE for an immediate danger on the line, and TRAINING TIME OUT for anything else that is unsafe or unclear. The signal is the word on the primary net, a whistle blast, and a raised closed fist.

On CEASE FIRE or TRAINING TIME OUT: firing stops, weapons go to Condition 4, everyone stands fast. The RSO confirms the line is safe and reports the reason to the OIC. The OIC resolves the issue and states out loud what changed before the event restarts. Training does not reconvene until the issue is resolved. Nobody is questioned or corrected for calling one, and the RSO says so on the line before the first relay each day.

## Pre execution checklist

- [ ] Range control opened the range and gave the current fire condition and surface danger zone.
- [ ] Lateral limit markers and the muzzle direction stakes are in and confirmed by the RSO.
- [ ] Lanes walked, holes and stumps marked with pickets.
- [ ] Comms check complete with range control and the battalion COC on both nets.
- [ ] Corpsman present with the trauma bag, cooling gear, and litter; evacuation vehicle staged with driver present.
- [ ] Wet bulb reading taken and the work and rest cycle posted.
- [ ] Two water sources full at the line.
- [ ] Line safeties assigned, one per four firers, sectors confirmed, each one told the stop trigger.
- [ ] Ammunition drawn, counted, and held at the single issue point.
- [ ] Eye protection and double hearing protection inspected on every firer and every safety.
- [ ] Anything that changed since the sheet was signed named out loud: weather, personnel, the number of relays, a range control restriction, a substitution in the OIC or RSO billet. If a change adds a hazard the sheet does not carry, it gets added and the sheet goes back for signature.
