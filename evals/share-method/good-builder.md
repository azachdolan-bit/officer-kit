# Report Drill BUILDER: Instructions for Claude

**What this is.** A build specification. It contains no course material of any kind. You supply your own source, a student handout or clear photos of a report card, and Claude reads your source and builds you a drill file for a voice chat. Everything in the output comes from your material; nothing comes from here.

**Keep it yours.** What Claude builds from your material is your own study product. Your own school's or unit's integrity policy governs who you may share it with; read that policy before you pass the product on. This file is what you share.

**How to use it.**

1. Gather your source: a handout, or photos of a report card, one report at a time.
2. Open a new Claude chat. Attach your source and this file.
3. Say: "Follow the builder instructions and build my drill file."
4. Check the transcription Claude reads back to you before it builds anything.
5. Answer the questions it asks, then save the file it produces.
6. To practice, open a voice chat, attach the drill file, and say "run me a rep."

# INSTRUCTIONS FOR CLAUDE

You are building a drill file that a voice chat will later use to run the student through a report, play the station that receives it, and grade the result.

## Prerequisite

The source the student supplied is your only authority. Do not supply any term, value, step, format rule, or option from general knowledge, from another publication, or from memory of how this is done elsewhere. Where the source differs from what you expect, the source is right.

If no source is attached, stop and ask for it. Do not offer an interim version.

## Working from photographs

Establish how many reports are in the images and what order the photos go in. Transcribe everything visible, verbatim. Never guess at anything you cannot read: mark it unreadable and ask for a better photo. Keep printed text and handwritten notes as two layers and ask which one is authoritative. Then read the whole transcription back and wait for the student to confirm it before you build.

## Step 1: Classify what you are building

Decide whether the source is a fixed format report or a back and forth procedure, and say which you concluded and why.

## Step 2: Extract exhaustively

Write down every item in these categories exactly as the source states it: every line in order with its label; what each line holds and in what form; every option list with the criteria for each option; every convention for numbers, including rounding and any tie rule; every threshold, including any case where the right action is to send nothing; anything marked not tested; and every worked example, kept apart as the test set. Common mistakes come from a skipped category, not a skipped item.

## Step 3: Verify before writing

Re-derive every worked example using only the rules you extracted. If one fails, the rule is wrong. Weight tie cases and the cases where nothing is sent. Report how many examples you tested.

## Step 4: Build the product

Write one drill file: a menu of reports, the rules reference, three levels (prompted, numbered, cold), turns of no more than fifteen seconds of speech, a coach who names the error without giving the answer, and scenario values built backwards from the outcome so they never contradict the rules.

## Step 5: Deliver

Give the student the file. Name every item you marked unreadable or could not source, and every ambiguity. Do not fill a gap with material you could not find.
