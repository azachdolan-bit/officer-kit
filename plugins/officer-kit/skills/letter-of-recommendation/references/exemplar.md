# Letter of recommendation exemplars, fictional, annotated

Every name, unit, and number is invented. The shape is drawn from letters that carried a Marine through a commissioning program board.

## Strong (six paragraphs, one page)

Subj: LETTER OF RECOMMENDATION FOR SERGEANT J. M. OKAFOR IN SUPPORT OF THE MARINE ENLISTED COMMISSIONING EDUCATION PROGRAM

1. I served as Sergeant Okafor's Commanding Officer from July 2024 to July 2025 while she was the Chemical, Biological, Radiological, and Nuclear Defense Chief of Marine Wing Support Squadron 000, a squadron of roughly 600 Marines and Sailors. I observed her directly, in garrison and for five months deployed.

2. Sergeant Okafor is recommended without reservation for selection to the Marine Enlisted Commissioning Education Program. She is the best sergeant of the approximately 45 I have commanded in two commands over four years, and the only one to whom I gave a staff noncommissioned officer's billet and never had to take it back.

3. She held the CBRN Chief billet, a gunnery sergeant's billet, for the full year as a sergeant. In that time she trained 550 personnel across four units on deployment, raised the squadron's CBRN readiness from 71 to 94 percent current, and accounted for $1.2 million of defense equipment through a change of command inventory with zero discrepancies. She completed 15 credits toward her degree while deployed.

4. In February 2025 the squadron's decontamination equipment failed a readiness inspection two days before a certification exercise. Sergeant Okafor identified the fault, sourced replacement components from the adjacent squadron, retrained the operators overnight, and the section passed the exercise on schedule. I did not direct any of it; I learned of it from the inspector.

5. If selected, I expect Sergeant Okafor to lead a platoon the way she led her section: by knowing the work better than the people she is leading and by setting a standard she meets first. If not selected this cycle, she will reapply, and I will write this letter again.

6. My point of contact is Major A. B. Example at (000) 000-0000 or a.b.example@usmc.mil.

Annotations. Paragraph 1 states standing and direct observation, with the scale of the unit. Paragraph 2 recommends in the strongest form the writer means and ranks with a denominator (45, two commands, four years) and one fact that proves the ranking. Paragraph 3 is four numbers, each with a period. Paragraph 4 is a dated scene and ends with the line a board trusts most: the CO did not direct it. Paragraph 5 makes no new claim. No word on the strike list appears.

## Weak (annotated)

1. It is my distinct honor and privilege to recommend Sergeant Okafor for MECEP. [Ceremony. Standing is missing: who is the writer, over what period?]

2. Sergeant Okafor is an outstanding Marine with a take charge attitude who has been the heart and soul of the section since her arrival. She is truly among the best I have served with. [Four adjectives and a ranking with no denominator. "Among the best" of how many, over what years?]

3. She has completed numerous courses and has consistently gone above and beyond in every task assigned. Her PFT and CFT scores are first class. [Data sheet material. No number a board cannot find on the next page.]

4. I have no doubt she will excel as an officer. [No scene. The writer saw something; it is not on the page.]

5. Please contact me with any questions. [No POC.]

Rewrite: paragraph 1 states the writer's billet and dates; paragraph 2 keeps the recommendation and asks the writer for the denominator; paragraph 3 replaces "numerous" with the count and adds the two numbers from the billet; paragraph 4 becomes the February scene; the POC is added.
