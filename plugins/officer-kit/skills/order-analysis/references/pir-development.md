# PIR development

Sources, all in the user's library (kit standard 5): MCWP 2-10, Intelligence Operations (6 October 2021); MCRP 2-10B.1, Intelligence Preparation of the Battlespace; MCWP 5-10, Marine Corps Planning Process; MCTP 2-10A, MAGTF Intelligence Collection (Change 1, 4 April 2018). Page numbers are from those editions; cite the user's copy. The user's school or unit material wins where it differs (Overrides first).

## Where PIRs come from

1. IPB Step 4, Determine Adversary COAs, produces the enemy situation templates, COA statements, event templates, event matrices, and the initial PIRs and IRs. COA War Game produces the PIRs. (MCRP 2-10B.1, figure 2-1, p. 2-2)
2. The event template starts from the situation template. NAIs go where the enemy COAs differ. Indicators are the actions that confirm or deny each COA. (MCRP 2-10B.1, pp. 6-35 to 6-36)
3. The event matrix lists each NAI with its indicators, the times from the time phase lines, and the enemy decision points. (MCRP 2-10B.1, p. 6-40)
4. Each CCIR asks a question that, when answered, assists the commander in making a key decision. Planners link each PIR to a specific decision and its branch plan or sequel. (MCWP 5-10, p. 2-17 and p. 3-2)
5. A PIR is tied to a critical decision and usually to an important planning assumption; an IR is not. The enemy's most likely and most dangerous COAs are the usual assumptions a commander validates with PIRs. The commander approves every PIR. The lowest priority PIR outranks the highest priority IR. (MCWP 2-10, pp. 2-5 to 2-6)

## A good PIR or IR (MCWP 2-10, p. 2-6)

- Asks only one question.
- Asks for specific facts, events, or activities concerning the enemy or the operational environment.
- Is tied to a specific unit, time, terrain, decision point, or target.
- Contains geographic and time elements to limit the scope of the requirement.

MCTP 2-10A (p. 2-3) gives five characteristics: it adds "tied to mission planning, decisionmaking, and execution" and "a clear, concise statement of what intelligence is required." MCWP 2-10 is the newer publication. Use its list; name the MCTP 2-10A list when the user's school teaches it.

Doctrine example (MCWP 2-10, p. 2-6): "Will the 84th Artillery Battalion emplace artillery forward phase line red prior to D-day?"

## From PIR to collection (MCWP 2-10, pp. 3-5 to 3-7)

1. **Identify, validate, and prioritize** the PIRs and IRs.
2. **Identify indicators:** observable activities related to the event, each associated with an NAI. Negative information counts.
3. **Derive SIRs:** break each indicator into specific questions. A complete SIR states the information required, where (the NAI), when (planned back from the LTIOV), the reporting criteria, and the recipients.
4. **Develop the SOR:** restate each SIR as a directive to a collection asset or resource. An SOR to a subordinate is an order; to another command, a request.

MCWP 2-10 p. 3-7 carries a sample SIR and its two SORs.

## The product

One block per PIR, highest priority first: the question; the decision it supports; the enemy COA or assumption it confirms or denies; where; when, with the LTIOV; its indicators with their NAIs; the SIRs under each indicator; the SOR under each SIR with the asset it tasks. Every element comes from the order, the user's IPB products, or the commander's guidance. A missing NAI, time, or decision point is an RFI.
