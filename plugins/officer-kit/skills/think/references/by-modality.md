# By modality

The estimate and the check are the same everywhere. What changes is which failure dominates, and therefore which pass runs first and hardest.

## Risk

**The dominant failure is a short hazard list.** Nothing mechanical can detect it. The arithmetic can be perfect on a worksheet that never named the hazard that hurt someone.

So the premortem runs **before** the hazard list, not after the draft. "Three Marines were hurt on this event. How?" produces a longer and more specific list than "what are the hazards," because the first question is about this event and the second is about a category.

Then, on the list: what hazard appears on every worksheet for an event like this that is not on mine? Heat in August. The road, not the range. The transition between serials, where the negligent discharges happen. Night. The drive home afterward.

Then the fourth assumption question from MCWP 5-10, applied to the controls: does this control assume away the thing most likely to fail? A control that rests on one person who also has three other jobs assumes away the most common failure. A control that depends on equipment arriving assumes away the most common cancellation.

The deliberate estimate is mandatory here and cannot be lowered. The delivery names what nothing checked: the completeness of the hazard list, and whether the people named will be there.

## Planning and orders

**The dominant failure is an unstated assumption that the plan rests on.** MCWP 5-10 Appendix G already maps the techniques to the steps, so the kit follows the Marine Corps' own map rather than inventing one:

| Step | Techniques the order names |
|---|---|
| Problem framing | Four Ways of Seeing, Key Assumptions Check, Frame Audit |
| Course of action development | Team A / Team B, Premortem Analysis, Devil's Advocate, Stakeholder Mapping |
| Course of action war game | Devil's Advocate, SWOT, Functional Systems Approach |
| Comparison and decision | Devil's Advocate, Key Assumptions Check |
| Orders development | Analysis of Competing Hypotheses, Cultural Perception Framework, Divergent/Convergent Analysis |
| Transition | Four Ways of Seeing, Outside-In Thinking, Five Whys |

**Four Ways of Seeing**, whenever another unit, a partner, or an adversary is in the problem, is four cells and all four get filled: how we see ourselves, how we think they see us, how they see themselves, how they see us. Empty cells are the finding.

**Five whys** is in the order and is weak as usually taught, because a single chain produces one root cause and a different analyst gets a different one. Run it as a tree: at each level, more than one why, and stop at a cause the unit can actually change.

The order's own disposition rule applies to the product: plans may carry assumptions, orders should not, and unresolved assumptions carried into execution are risk. Anything unresolved is named in the transition, not buried in paragraph 3.

## Writing that enters a record

**The dominant failure is a sentence that does not say what the writer meant, in a document nobody can amend later.** Three passes, in this order.

**Tagging.** Every sentence is one of three things: information the user supplied, an assumption, or a judgment. The intelligence community's analytic standards require the distinction and it is the fastest way to find the invented sentence. In an award summary, "trained 550 Marines" is information; "which closed the section's shortfall" is a judgment that needs either a source or a flag; anything that is neither is a gap to ask about.

The same standards prohibit combining a likelihood and a confidence in one statement, which is worth carrying: "it is highly likely, with high confidence" says less than either half alone.

**Adversarial.** Every directive sentence, read for its most inconvenient compliant meaning. This is the pass that turns "report daily" into "not later than 0740 on every duty day," and it is the difference between a Page 11 that supports a separation and one that does not.

**Structure.** `precision_check.py`. Center embedding first, because it is the measured cause of difficulty in professional prose. Then sentence length against the 15 word target, paragraphs past 10 lines, unquantified quantifiers, passive directives with no actor, undefined acronyms.

For products with prescribed wording (the Page 11 entry, award citation openings and closings, the naval letter's heading block), the structure pass never touches the prescribed text. The order's words are the order's words; the pass applies to what the writer supplies between them.

## Investigation and inquiry

**The dominant failure is committing to one explanation early and then collecting evidence for it.** This is the one place in the kit where Analysis of Competing Hypotheses belongs, because the JAGMAN's structure already demands it and because the cost of the failure is somebody's record.

Adapted, and honestly labeled: controlled tests do not show ACH improving accuracy, so it is used here for what it demonstrably does, which is force disconfirmation and diagnosticity into the record.

1. List the hypotheses before writing findings. At least three, including "no one did anything wrong and the system produced this."
2. List the evidence, then ask of each item which hypotheses it is consistent with. Evidence consistent with every hypothesis is not diagnostic and does not carry weight, however compelling it reads.
3. Work to disconfirm rather than confirm. The strongest hypothesis is the one with the least evidence against it, not the one with the most for it.
4. Ask what evidence would exist under each hypothesis and is absent. Absence that should not be there is a finding.
5. Check the quality of each source before a finding rests on it: who observed it, when, how, and what would make them wrong.

The tool never selects the hypothesis. The investigating officer does, and the report shows the ones that were considered and set aside, which is what makes the reasoning reviewable.

## Evaluation

Awards level checks, fitness report marks, promotion and reenlistment recommendations.

**The dominant failure is anchoring on the requested outcome.** A package written to a level rather than to the facts is the thing the awards order specifically warns about, and a mark chosen before the evidence is assembled is the thing the performance evaluation manual warns about.

So the requested outcome is quarantined. The criteria assessment runs against the facts and the manual **without the requested level in view**, and only then is the request compared to the assessment. Practically: assemble and read back the fact list, then read the criteria, then say what level the facts reach, and only then say whether that matches what was asked for.

The same quarantine applies to the user's stated preference in any evaluative product. Their preference is a fact about what they want, recorded and honored in the decision; it is not evidence about what the facts support, and it does not enter the paragraph where the facts are weighed.

Where the assessment and the request differ, the tool says so once with the criterion quoted, then drafts what the user directs and records the gap in the fact list so the signer sees both.
