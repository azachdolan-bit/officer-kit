Keeping a Sourdough Starter (fictional handout written for this example, and its content is not to be taken into any real product)

Chapter 1. What a starter is
A sourdough starter is a culture of wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria living in a paste of flour and water.
The yeast produces the gas that raises the bread, and the bacteria produce the acids that give it a sour taste.
A healthy starter doubles in volume between four and eight hours after it is fed at room temperature.
A starter that does not double within twelve hours is called sluggish and needs two days of regular feeding before it is used.

Chapter 2. Feeding
Feeding means discarding most of the starter and mixing the rest with fresh flour and water.
The standard feed is one part starter, one part flour, and one part water by weight, written as 1:1:1.
A stiff feed of 1:2:1 slows fermentation and is used when the starter will sit for more than a day.
Always use water below 30 degrees Celsius, because hotter water kills the yeast.
Feed a starter kept at room temperature once every twenty four hours.
Feed a starter kept in the refrigerator once every seven days.

Chapter 3. Reading the signs
A layer of gray liquid on top, called hooch, means the starter is hungry and should be fed sooner.
Pink or orange streaks mean the culture is contaminated and the whole starter must be thrown away.
A smell of nail polish remover means the starter is overdue for a feed but is still safe to use.
The float test drops a spoonful of starter into water; if it floats, the starter is ready to bake with.
