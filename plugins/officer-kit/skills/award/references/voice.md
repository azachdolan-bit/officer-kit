# Award voice: how approved citations and summaries read

Drawn from approved products and the manual's standard sentences. The plugin's own examples are fictional.

## Standard sentences (confirm against SECNAV M-1650.1 Appendix 2E Table 21 in the user's copy)

Opening, Achievement Medal, meritorious: `FOR PROFESSIONAL ACHIEVEMENT IN THE SUPERIOR PERFORMANCE OF HIS DUTIES WHILE SERVING AS <BILLET>, <UNIT>, FROM <MONTH YEAR> TO <MONTH YEAR>.`

Opening, Commendation Medal, meritorious: `FOR MERITORIOUS ACHIEVEMENT WHILE SERVING AS <BILLET>, <UNIT>, FROM <MONTH YEAR> TO <MONTH YEAR>` and, on a deployment, `, IN SUPPORT OF OPERATION <NAME>.`

Closing, both: `<GRADE> <LAST NAME>'S INITIATIVE, PERSEVERANCE, AND TOTAL DEDICATION TO DUTY REFLECTED CREDIT UPON HIM AND WERE IN KEEPING WITH THE HIGHEST TRADITIONS OF THE MARINE CORPS AND THE UNITED STATES NAVAL SERVICE.` ("Devotion to duty" is also seen. "Her" and "herself" as appropriate.)

Between the opening and the closing: two to four sentences, each carrying a number and a consequence, in the order of significance.

## Verbs that carry facts

led, trained, instructed, coordinated, planned, executed, built, identified, corrected, secured, raised, reduced, tracked, licensed, certified, supervised, established, recovered, delivered.

## Words that do the work facts should do (strike on sight)

enthusiastically, exceptional, exceptionally, outstanding, superb, phenomenal, epitome, epitomizes, go to, take charge attitude, above and beyond, consistent above and beyond, herculean, unmatched, unparalleled, level headed, dedicated (as an adjective), motivated, hard work, tireless, selfless (unless the act was), instrumental (without saying to what), pivotal (without saying to what), vital, invaluable.

Test: delete the word. If the sentence loses a fact, keep it. If it loses only volume, it was fluff.

## Professionalism rules

- Grade and last name after the first full mention; never a first name alone; no nicknames.
- No abbreviations a general officer's reader outside the unit would not know. Spell out the unit at first mention.
- Numbers as numerals when they are counts; spelled out only at the start of a sentence.
- One claim per sentence. No semicolons in a citation.
- Nothing about the Marine's personal life beyond the volunteer paragraph, and nothing medical, financial, or family.
- The tone is a senior officer stating facts about a subordinate to another senior officer. Not a eulogy, not a recruiting poster.
