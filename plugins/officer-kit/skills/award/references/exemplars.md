# Award exemplars, fictional, annotated

Every name, unit, number, and date here is invented. The shapes are drawn from approved products. Read the annotations, not just the text.

## Exemplar 1. Achievement Medal, specific achievement (strong)

**Summary of Action (bullets, one page)**

- Rifle platoon commander, Company C, 1st Battalion, 3d Marines, January to June 2026. Held the billet as a second lieutenant for the full period.
- Led the platoon's rifle qualification across two ranges in five days; qualification rose from 78 to 96 percent, the highest in the battalion for the cycle (battalion average 85). [before and after, comparison, source: range control roster]
- Wrote the company fire support plan used without change in the regimental exercise in April; the plan integrated two 81 mm sections and a battery for 14 targets. [scope, result: adopted as written]
- When the contracted range equipment provider cancelled two weeks before the live fire progression, secured replacement equipment from the adjacent battalion and executed the progression on the original dates for 42 Marines with no safety incidents. [what broke, action, count, result]
- Coached the unit youth soccer program for 30 hours over the period. [outside the billet, one line, counted]

Annotation: the first bullet establishes the billet and period; every following bullet has a number and a consequence; the hardest thing (the cancelled provider) is stated as problem, action, result; nothing is characterized. A reader can verify each line against a roster, a plan, or a calendar.

**Citation**

FOR PROFESSIONAL ACHIEVEMENT IN THE SUPERIOR PERFORMANCE OF HIS DUTIES WHILE SERVING AS RIFLE PLATOON COMMANDER, COMPANY C, 1ST BATTALION, 3D MARINES, FROM JANUARY 2026 TO JUNE 2026. SECOND LIEUTENANT VANCE LED HIS PLATOON'S RIFLE QUALIFICATION TO 96 PERCENT, THE HIGHEST IN THE BATTALION FOR THE CYCLE, AND WROTE THE COMPANY FIRE SUPPORT PLAN ADOPTED WITHOUT CHANGE FOR THE REGIMENTAL EXERCISE. WHEN THE RANGE EQUIPMENT PROVIDER CANCELLED TWO WEEKS OUT, HE SECURED REPLACEMENT EQUIPMENT AND EXECUTED THE LIVE FIRE PROGRESSION FOR 42 MARINES ON THE ORIGINAL DATES WITHOUT INCIDENT. SECOND LIEUTENANT VANCE'S INITIATIVE, PERSEVERANCE, AND TOTAL DEDICATION TO DUTY REFLECTED CREDIT UPON HIM AND WERE IN KEEPING WITH THE HIGHEST TRADITIONS OF THE MARINE CORPS AND THE UNITED STATES NAVAL SERVICE.

Annotation: standard opening and closing; three sentences between them, each a fact from the SOA with the same numbers (96, 42, two weeks); no adjectives; within 8 lines at Times New Roman 10. Every number here appears in the SOA above.

## Exemplar 2. Achievement Medal, sustained performance (strong)

**Summary of Action (paragraphs; the command prefers them)**

Corporal Ashe is recommended for the Navy and Marine Corps Achievement Medal for sustained superior performance as a supply clerk, Headquarters Company, 2d Marine Regiment, from March 2025 to March 2026.

Over the period, Corporal Ashe processed 1,140 requisitions with a 99.6 percent accuracy rate on the quarterly audits, against a section average of 96 percent, and reduced the average requisition turnaround from nine days to four by building a tracking sheet the section adopted in June and still uses. He identified $212,000 of serialized gear carried on the wrong account during the change of command inventory, corrected the records, and the inventory closed with zero discrepancies for the first time in three years.

As the section's junior Marine training NCO, he taught the supply system to 11 new Marines in four classes and each qualified on the system within 30 days of arrival, against a unit standard of 60.

Corporal Ashe volunteered 40 hours with the base Toys for Tots drive in November and December 2025.

Annotation: sustained performance is proven by rates and comparisons (99.6 against 96; nine days to four; 30 days against 60), each with a source a reader could ask for. The dollar figure and "first time in three years" are the two facts a board remembers. Four paragraphs, under one page.

## Exemplar 3. Commendation Medal on a deployment (strong, and the level argued honestly)

**Summary of Action (paragraphs, two pages maximum)**

Staff Sergeant Rios is recommended for the Navy and Marine Corps Commendation Medal for meritorious achievement as Detachment Training Chief, Combat Logistics Detachment 31, Special Purpose Marine Air Ground Task Force, from April 2025 to March 2026, in support of Operation Example. He held a billet normally filled by a gunnery sergeant for the full period.

In the pre deployment training period, Staff Sergeant Rios scheduled and tracked 64 training events for 271 Marines and raised the detachment's training readiness rating from T-3 to T-1 with 93 percent of personnel current, verified in the readiness reporting system in August 2025. He identified that the training system did not carry the 23 attached Marines from a supporting unit, built a local tracker reconciled against 300 rosters across three systems, and the commander used it for every readiness brief thereafter.

Deployed, he tailored quarterly CBRN refresher training to four units and trained 541 personnel without interrupting crisis response operations. He trained 12 unit training representatives with no prior experience to run decentralized training; with that structure, 183 Marines completed all 15 actionable annual training events before redeployment and returned able to lead the next cycle's training plan.

Annotation: this is the level argument done right. The first paragraph states the billet above grade. Every paragraph names a problem, the action, the number, and the effect on more than one unit, which is what separates a Commendation Medal from an Achievement Medal in the manual's terms (scope beyond the Marine's own section; effect on the command). The tool still tells the recommender: a board may approve this as an Achievement Medal, and nothing on the page can prevent that; what the page can do is make the scope unmistakable.

## Exemplar 4. Weak (annotated line by line)

**Summary of Action, as submitted**

Lance Corporal Penn is enthusiastically recommended for the Navy and Marine Corps Achievement Medal for his outstanding performance as a motor transport operator. [Grade, billet, unit, and period are missing. "Enthusiastically" and "outstanding" are the recommender's mood, not facts.]

Lance Corporal Penn is a go to Marine who always goes above and beyond. He is the first to volunteer and the last to leave. [Zero facts. A board cannot verify a work ethic.]

He was instrumental in the success of the battalion's field exercise and played a pivotal role in the motor pool. [Instrumental to what, pivotal in what? No number, no event named, no result.]

He drove many missions without incident and kept his vehicles in great shape. [How many missions, how many miles, over what period, what readiness rate? "Great shape" is not a maintenance figure.]

Lance Corporal Penn is the epitome of a Marine and is highly deserving of this award. [The conclusion restates the request. The manual's criteria are never touched.]

**What the intake would have produced instead**

- Motor transport operator, Truck Company, 1st Battalion, 6th Marines, September 2025 to March 2026.
- Drove 47 convoy missions totaling 3,900 miles in support of the battalion field exercise and two range periods, without a mishap or a late departure.
- Maintained a four vehicle section at 100 percent operational readiness for the period against a company average of 88 percent, documented in the monthly readiness report.
- Identified a recurring brake fault on two vehicles, wrote the deficiency report, and the fix was applied across the company's 24 vehicles.

Annotation: same Marine, same period, and now a board has four facts it can check. Whether that reaches an Achievement Medal is a judgment the recommender makes with the manual open; the tool's job was to get the facts on the page and strip the adjectives that hid their absence.
