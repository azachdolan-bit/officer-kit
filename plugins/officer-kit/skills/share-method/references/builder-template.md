# INSTRUCTIONS FOR CLAUDE

You are building <the product> from the source the student attached. <One sentence on what the product must do when it is used.>

## Prerequisite

The source the student supplied is your only authority. Do not supply any term, value, step, format rule, or option from general knowledge, from another publication, or from memory of how this is done elsewhere. Many subjects have a widely known textbook version, and the version a given school or unit uses often differs; where the source differs from what you expect, the source is right. Never normalize toward the familiar version.

If no source is attached, stop and ask for it. Do not offer to build an interim version from general knowledge.

## Working from photographs

Establish scope first: how many distinct items are in the images, and in what order the photos go. Do not infer order from file names.

Transcribe everything visible, verbatim: every label, number, unit, abbreviation, and marginal note, in the source's own capitalization and numbering.

Never guess at anything you cannot read. Mark it unreadable and ask for a better photo of that area.

Keep printed text and handwritten annotations as two layers, and ask which one is authoritative.

Then read the whole transcription back and stop. Ask the student to confirm it with the card in front of them, calling out every number, every abbreviation that differs from another by one letter, the order of lines or steps, every option list and whether it is complete, and everything marked unreadable. Do not build until they confirm.

## Step 2: Extract exhaustively

Write down everything in the categories below exactly as the source states it. Do not summarize, do not skip an item because it seems obvious, and do not hunt for particular items: completeness is the method.

<Categories, each a kind of thing rather than an instance: structure and order; what each field holds and in what form; every option list with its criteria; every number convention, threshold, and formula, including any case where the right action is to do nothing; exclusions the source marks as not tested; every worked example, collected separately as the test set.>

## Step 3: Verify before writing

Re-derive every worked example in the source using only the rules you extracted. All must reproduce; if one does not, the rule is wrong, so fix the rule. Weight tie cases and cases where the right answer is to send or do nothing. If the source has no worked examples, build one yourself, check it field by field against the source, and say plainly that the file was verified for internal consistency only. Report how many examples you tested and whether any failed.

Then list every place the rules are silent or ambiguous: a threshold that does not say whether it applies before or after rounding, an option with no rule for choosing it, a case two rules both claim. Keep every invented value out of those places, and name each one to the student at delivery.

Where covering every option would mean inventing a rule the source does not give, the source wins: leave that option out of invented values and name it as a gap.

<Interactive page only:> The page carries a self test: a button that drives the page's real input engine through every item end to end and reports the pass count. Do not deliver until it reports 100 percent with 0 errors, and say so in your message.

## Step 5: Deliver

Give the student the file. In your message, state what you verified and how, name every item you marked unreadable or could not source, flag anything the source left ambiguous, and list what you left out because the source marked it not tested. Do not fill a gap with material you could not find: a gap the student knows about is safer than an invention they do not.
