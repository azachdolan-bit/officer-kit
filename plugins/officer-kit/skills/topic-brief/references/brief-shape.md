# Brief shape

The brief is what the speaker knows cold; the script is what they say. `brief_check.py --kind brief` reads the headings and lines named here.

```
# <Title>

Topic: <the topic exactly as assigned>

<Opening paragraph: the topic sentence and the answer, in plain words. It names the topic.>

## Context
<One paragraph, 120 words or fewer: only what the listener needs to follow the topic. No second context section anywhere.>

## <A heading that names what this group of incidents illustrates>
<Dated incidents, each tied to the topic in the same sentence. Every number with its source and its scope.>

## <Next thing the incidents illustrate>
...

## <The doctrinal element, if the assignment asks for one>
<Quoted from the user's library copy, with the chapter, and applied to the incidents above.>

## Where every fact comes from
| Line | Source |
|---|---|
| <each fact or number as used> | <author, title, chapter or page; a link when there is one> |

## Lines not to say
- "<the tempting line>" <why: which sources disagree, or that no source supports it>

## Gaps
- <what could not be found or read, and where it would be>
```

## Rules

1. **Topic first.** The opening paragraph names the topic and answers it. Timeline order is for the incidents inside a group, not for the brief.
2. **Group by what it illustrates,** not by date. "The attack aimed at the seam" is a heading; "8 March" is not.
3. **Every number answers the topic** in the sentence it appears in, and carries its scope: whose count, what period, what unit. A two day regimental casualty figure is never said about one night.
4. **Disagreement is written down,** in lines not to say, with the sources named. The line said in its place is the one true under all of them.
5. **Memory is said as memory.** Dialogue recalled decades later is "he later told his biographer," never a transcript.
6. **Read the source, not the compiler.** A research file's heading or framing line ("dusk", "same night, same stretch of line") is the compiler's claim, not the source's; grade it that way. Check the echelon on every figure: a regimental combat team is not the regiment, a battalion's experience is not every Marine's.
7. **A diary's time** may mark when something started, not the moment the talk ties it to.
8. **Every acronym** is one the audience knows or is spelled out once.
