# Memo intake

One question at a time. "I do not know" is recorded as a gap, never filled. Ask what this paper raises, not what staff papers usually raise. Never put a candidate answer inside the question.

## 1. Which paper
1. What is this for: to record something that happened so it is in the file (memorandum for the record); to hand a senior the facts before a meeting (information paper); to carry the key points as a reminder (point paper); to get a decision (position/decision paper); to speak from at a meeting (talking paper)? The publication defines each; one purpose, one paper. If the user wants a memorandum between offices with From and To lines, that is SECNAV M-5216.5, not in the library; say so and offer the MFR or a naval letter.
2. Who reads it and what will they do with it? (For a decision paper: what exactly is the decision, as a yes or no the reader can sign?)

## 2. The facts
3. For an MFR: what happened, when, who was present by billet, what was said or decided, and what action is now underway and why? An MFR records; it does not argue.
4. For an information or point paper: what does the reader need to know walking in that they do not know now? What are the facts, with numbers and dates? What could go wrong in the meeting that the reader should see coming?
5. For a decision paper: what is the problem, why is a decision needed now, what has happened before, what do other sections or agencies hold (or "Not Applicable"), what position is recommended and on what reasoning, and what is the fallback?
6. Which references were actually used as sources? (They are cited in the discussion, not listed for show.)

## 3. Form
7. Originator code and date (the day of dispatch for an information paper). For an MFR the signer's name, billet, and grade and service as Appendix D prints them; for a point or decision paper the preparer and approving official by grade, billet, and phone; for an information paper the preparer only. Identity from the rules file; nothing guessed.
8. Classification: unclassified papers carry no markings; the publication's samples show where (U) portion marks go if the paper is ever classified. Confirm unclassified.
9. Length: a point or information paper is one page (two at most for an information or decision paper, by the publication). What must be cut or moved to a tab to hold that?

## 4. Correlate to the standard
10. Hold the draft against the appendix for that paper: every required part present in the publication's order and heading words; the recommendation one the reader can approve or disapprove with a signature; nothing the reader would have to look up.

## 5. The reader
11. How does this staff want papers written (paragraphs or ticks; who approves; whether the commander wants the recommendation first)? Record in `Overrides/memo.md`.

## The quantification ladder
| Rung | Question |
|---|---|
| Action | What happened, or what is asked? |
| Scope | When, for how many, over what period? |
| Result | What was decided or found, in a number where there is one? |
| Comparison | Against the plan, the standard, or last time? |
| Consequence | What does the reader now need to do, and by when? |
Stop at the rung the writer can defend from the record.
