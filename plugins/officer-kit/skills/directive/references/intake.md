# Directive intake

One question at a time. "I do not know" is recorded as a gap, never filled. Ask what this directive raises, not what directives usually raise. Never put a candidate answer inside the question.

## 1. Whether it is a directive at all, and which type
1. What must the reader do differently after this is signed, and for how long? (The answer picks the type. Continuing authority meant as a permanent reference is an order, chapter 1 paragraph 3.b. A one time or brief term requirement of 12 months or less is a bulletin, 3.c. The order knows no third type; a "standing operating procedure" that is a permanent reference is an order in the five paragraph format, and a letter of instruction is not a directive at all, paragraph 4.i.)
2. Is the subject already covered completely by a higher directive? (Paragraph 5.b: no supplement when the higher directive provides complete details; clarification and implementation guidance only, and the higher directive incorporated by reference.)
3. What echelon issues it: company, battery, detachment, battalion, squadron, or higher? Is the command separately detached or an Inspector-Instructor staff? (Paragraph 5.c bars supplements below battalion or squadron on leave and liberty, assumption of command, alcoholic beverage control, mail handling, and command security procedures, with that one exception.)
4. Who is the principal official, by title, whose title goes in the "From:" line? (Chapter 1 paragraph 3.e; paragraph 6: directives are issued under the title of the principal official.)

## 2. Identification
5. What SSIC does the sponsor propose, and who at the command's Directives Control Point verifies it? (Paragraph 21.b. The SSIC manual is not in the library; the tool records the number given and who verifies it.)
6. For an order: has the DCP assigned the consecutive point number, or is it to be assigned on signature? Is this a revision of an existing order, and if so what letter follows? (Paragraph 21.d and 21.e; never I, O, or Q.)
7. For a bulletin: what is the cancellation month and year, and is cancellation contingent on an action or event? (Chapter 3 paragraph 2: "Canc:" for a given date, "Canc frp:" with a Cancellation Contingency last paragraph when contingent. Twelve months at most, chapter 1 paragraph 3.c.)
8. What is the command's designation line and abbreviation (for example the battalion's own order series), and the sponsor code? (Basic order 4.a(1)(b)2; figure 2-2.)
9. Does the directive apply to the Marine Corps Reserve, to the Total Force, or to the command only? (Chapter 2 paragraph 5.a; chapter 3 paragraph 11.e; paragraph 22 for a Reserve only directive.)

## 3. Cancellation and references
10. Does this cancel any directive the command sponsors? Which, by identification, and for a bulletin, the date of the basic bulletin? (Chapter 2 paragraph 3.b and 3.c; chapter 3 paragraph 11.b. Only directives you sponsor.)
11. Which references does the text actually use, in the order they first appear? Any bulletin or MARADMIN among them carries its cancellation date. (Paragraph 24.b, 24.e.)
12. Which enclosures, in the order they first appear, with the exact title of each? (Paragraph 26.)
13. Does the directive impose a report, prescribe a form, or require records to be kept? (Paragraphs 29, 30, 31: a Reports Required section, a Forms paragraph, and a records disposition instruction follow from yes.)

## 4. The five paragraphs (order) or the bulletin paragraphs
14. Situation: what is the purpose, in one or two sentences? (Chapter 2 paragraph 3.a.)
15. Mission: what task, for whom, where, when, and why? (3.c; figure 2-1 paragraph 3.)
16. Commander's Intent: the commander's own expression of the purpose and the end state. Concept of Operations: how the commander plans to accomplish it. (Figure 2-1 paragraph 4a.)
17. Subordinate Element Missions: which element is the main effort, which support, what is the reserve, and what is each tasked to do, by billet? (3.d(2); figure 2-2 paragraph 3b.)
18. Coordinating Instructions: what applies to two or more elements? (3.d(3).)
19. Administration and Logistics: what support, what responsibilities, what records and how disposed of? (Chapter 2 paragraph 4; chapter 1 paragraph 31.)
20. For a bulletin: Purpose, then Cancellation if any, Background if needed, Action, Reserve Applicability, Cancellation Contingency last if contingent. (Chapter 3 paragraph 11.)

## 5. Signature and distribution
21. Who signs: the principal official, a subordinate by title under a written delegation, a further delegate by direction, or someone acting? (Chapter 1 paragraph 7 and 7.b; paragraph 37.a: name in capitals, no grade or rank, no title when the signer is the principal official, title when signing by title, "Acting" when acting.)
22. Which distribution statement, and what goes under "DISTRIBUTION:" and "Copy to:"? (Paragraph 19; paragraph 37.b and 37.c.)

## 6. Review
23. When is the annual review, who records it on NAVMC 10974 or the command's tracking system, and who is the DCP in writing? (Basic order 4.b(2)(d); chapter 1 paragraphs 8 and 14. Revise at 9 years; a command directives review within 1 year of a new commander.)

## 7. The reader
24. How does this command write its directives: any local order on directives, local abbreviation, sponsor codes, review cycle? Record it in `Overrides/directive.md`.

## The quantification ladder, applied to a tasking
| Rung | Question |
|---|---|
| Action | What must the element do? |
| Scope | For which Marines, gear, or events, how often? |
| Result | What condition exists when it is done? |
| Comparison | Against which standard: the higher directive, the unit's own, last year's? |
| Consequence | What does the command get, or lose if it is not done? |
Stop at the rung the commander will defend when a Marine asks why.
