# Directive exemplars, fictional, annotated

Form only. Every number, billet, SSIC, subject, and phrase here is invented and is not to be taken into a product; `directive_check.py` fails a draft that lifts a sentence from this file. Read this after the intake is done and the fact list is confirmed, never before, so it teaches shape and supplies no answers. The user's own approved directives in `Reference/Exemplars/directive/` beat this file.

## Strong: a company standing procedure, issued as a company order in the five paragraph format

```
# Directive
Type: Order   Echelon: company   Purpose: continuing procedure for drawing and turning in weapons at the company armory   Supplements: none
Identification: CoO 8000.2   Sponsor code: XO   Date signed: (Date Signed)
Signed by: the principal official (Commanding Officer)   DCP: battalion adjutant verifies the SSIC and assigns the point number
References: MCO 5215.1K w/Admin Ch 3 enclosure (1) chapter 1 paragraphs 5 to 7, 21, 32, 37; chapter 2; figure 2-2

## Letterhead page
UNIT LETTERHEAD

                                        CoO 8000.2
                                        XO
                                        (Date Signed)

COMPANY ORDER 8000.2

From:  Commanding Officer
To:    Distribution List

Subj:  COMPANY ARMORY DRAW AND TURN IN PROCEDURES

Ref:   (a) Battalion Order 8000.4A
       (b) Battalion Order 5210.1

Encl:  (1) Platoon Draw Order

DISTRIBUTION STATEMENT A: Approved for public release; distribution is unlimited.

## Body
1. Situation. To set the procedure by which platoons draw and turn in weapons and optics at the company armory, within the limits of reference (a).

2. Mission. Platoon commanders, platoon sergeants, and the company armorer draw and turn in weapons by platoon on the schedule in this Order so that the company can account for every serialized item at the close of each training day.

3. Execution

    a. Commander's Intent and Concept of Operations

        (1) Commander's Intent. The armory closes each day with the count on the armory board equal to the count in the consolidated memorandum receipt, and a discrepancy is known to the company commander the day it occurs, not the day the inventory finds it. End state: no weapon or optic is outside the armory without a signed weapons card, and none is inside without a matching card in the box.

        (2) Concept of Operations. Platoons draw in sequence by the training schedule, one platoon at the window at a time, and turn in by the same sequence in reverse. The armorer runs the window; the platoon sergeant runs the platoon's line; the company gunnery sergeant settles any count that does not match before the armory is secured.

    b. Subordinate Element Missions

        (1) Company armorer. Main effort. Opens the armory on the schedule published under paragraph 3c, issues by weapons card, and posts the running count on the armory board.

        (2) Platoon sergeants. Supporting effort. Form the platoon in the draw order given in enclosure (1), check each weapons card against the serial number on the weapon before the Marine leaves the window, and certify the platoon's draw and turn in on the platoon sheet.

        (3) Company gunnery sergeant. Reconciles the armory board with the platoon sheets at turn in and secures the armory. Reserve: the company police sergeant assumes the armorer's window duties when the armorer is absent.

    c. Coordinating Instructions

        (1) The draw schedule for the week is published with the training schedule by 1600 on the Thursday before.

        (2) A Marine who cannot produce the weapons card at turn in reports to the platoon sergeant, who reports to the company gunnery sergeant before the armory is secured.

        (3) No weapon is drawn for cleaning after 1700 without the executive officer's approval.

4. Administration and Logistics

    a. Platoon sheets and the daily armory board count are kept in the armory for the current fiscal year and disposed of per reference (b).

    b. Weapons cards are issued and replaced by the armorer; a lost card is reported in writing to the executive officer within one working day.

5. Command and Signal

    a. Command. This Order is applicable to all Marines and Sailors assigned or attached to the company.

    b. Signal. This Order is effective the date signed.

## Signature page
                                        <SIGNER_CAPS>

DISTRIBUTION: Platoon commanders, platoon sergeants, company gunnery sergeant, company armorer

Copy to: Battalion adjutant (directives file)

## Review
Annual review: the month of signature each year, recorded on NAVMC 10974 or the battalion's tracking system by the executive officer; revise when 9 years old or after the ninth change; command directives review within 1 year of a change of command. DCP: battalion adjutant, designated in writing by the battalion.
```

Annotation. The type follows from the purpose: continuing procedure, permanent reference, so an order in the five paragraph format under the company's own designation line, and the header says "Order", not "SOP", because the governing order knows no third type. Situation is first, Mission second (nothing is canceled, so no Cancellation paragraph and five paragraphs, not six). Execution carries the three subparagraphs the order prescribes with Commander's Intent and Concept of Operations broken out as (1) and (2), and the Subordinate Element Missions name the main effort, the supporting effort, and the reserve by billet. Every paragraph that is subdivided has at least two subdivisions. Both references and the enclosure are listed in the order they first appear and each is cited in the text. The Command subparagraph says to whom the Order applies in the shape the order gives, and the Signal sentence is the order's own words unaltered. The signature block is the label in capitals and nothing else: the signer is the principal official named in the From line, so no title and no rank. The distribution statement is on the letterhead page. The records instruction names the disposition reference. The review section gives the cycle the order imposes.

## Weak, annotated line by line

```
Type: SOP   Echelon: company
```
[The order issues directives as an Order or a Bulletin and no other type. If the content is a permanent reference it is an order; if it is a one time or brief term requirement it is a bulletin. The checker fails a third type.]

```
Subj:  COMPANY LIBERTY POLICY
```
[Chapter 1 paragraph 5.c: no supplement below battalion or squadron on leave and liberty, unless the command is separately detached or an Inspector-Instructor staff. A company order on this subject is barred.]

```
1. Purpose. This SOP sets forth the procedures all hands will follow.
2. Mission. ...
```
[An order opens with Situation, not Purpose; Purpose is the bulletin's first paragraph. "All hands" is on the strike list and "sets forth the procedures" says nothing.]

```
2. Mission. ... 3. Cancellation. CoO 3000.1.
```
[Cancellation is always the second paragraph if needed, with Mission third and six paragraphs in all.]

```
3. Execution
    a. Commander's Intent and Concept of Operations
    b. Subordinate Element Missions
```
[Commander's Intent and Concept of Operations are (1) and (2) under a, and Coordinating Instructions is c. A paragraph that is subdivided has at least two subdivisions.]

```
    b. Signal. This Order is effective 1 October 2026.
```
[The order prescribes "This Order is effective the date signed." An order that must wait is signed when it should take effect.]

```
                                        <SIGNER_CAPS>

                                        Commanding Officer

```
Canc: Dec 2028        (on a bulletin signed in Sep 2026)

[A bulletin remains in effect up to 12 months and no longer. A requirement that must last past that is an order.]
