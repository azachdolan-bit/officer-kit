# MROW input intake

One question at a time. "I do not know" is recorded as a gap, never filled. Ask what this reporting period raises, not what fitness reports usually raise. Never put a candidate answer inside the question. The user is the MRO writing about their own period; the tool asks for facts the RS can verify, because the RS will "assess the information provided by the MRO on the MROW and report that information on the MRO's fitness report as deemed appropriate" (chapter 2 paragraph 3.c(3)).

## 1. Frame
1. Which report is this input for: the occasion code (AN, CH, TD, and so on), and the period From and To as dates? (Chapter 1 paragraph 7.a: the summary of accomplishments is due to the RS "prior to the end of the reporting period".)
2. Your grade, and your billet as your rules file states it. If the rules file has no billet line, what billet did the RS and you formalize at the start of the period, and on what date did that meeting happen? (Chapter 1 paragraph 6.a: within the first 30 days.)
3. The RS's billet and the RO's billet. Not names; billets.
4. Did the billet description change during the period (a duty added, a duty dropped, a collateral picked up)? On what date, and did the RS discuss the change with you? (Chapter 4 paragraph 5.d.)
5. Is any part of the billet one the order calls unique (commander, executive officer, base or camp commander, student, instructor, aviator, planner)? Chapter 4 paragraph 3 lists what the RS is expected to comment on for each; the billet description should name those duties.

## 2. The billet description (section B as the MRO drafts it for the RS)
6. What are the primary duties, as the RS assigned them? Say what the billet is responsible for, not what the MOS qualifies you to do (paragraph 5.a: "should not restate the prerequisites of the Marine's MOS").
7. What additional and special duties did the RS assign (paragraph 5.c(2))? Which of those were collateral to the billet and which were separate assignments?
8. For each duty, what is the scope: how many Marines, how much equipment or money, how many events a year, how many units supported? A count is a scope statement, not a statistic; paragraph 5.c(3)(a) strikes "needless statistics", not numbers that define the job.
9. What standard did the RS set for each duty (paragraph 5.b(3): "acceptable standards vice goals"; paragraph 5.c(5): "Select the most significant standards; do not try to list them all")?

## 3. The accomplishments (section C as the MRO drafts it for the RS)
10. What did you accomplish in the billet this period? One at a time. For each, climb the ladder below and stop where the RS could verify it from records, counseling notes, or unit input (paragraph 6.b).
11. Which duty does each accomplishment relate to? Paragraph 6.b(2): items "must relate directly to assigned duties even though these responsibilities may not appear in section B". An accomplishment that relates to no assigned duty is a fact for the RS's information, not a section C item.
12. Which are the most significant? Paragraph 6.a(1) and 6.b(1) leave the prioritizing to the RS; your list should still be in your own order of significance so the RS knows which you would keep.
13. For each item: is this a result, or a description of you? "Led with energy" describes you; "trained 41 Marines to qualification in 6 weeks" is a result. Paragraph 6.a(4): "List only the results and achievements themselves and avoid all reference to personal qualities or potential impact".
14. Did any item happen while you were on a selection board or a court-martial? That item comes out (paragraph 6.b Note).
15. Any item that would release classified information? The word "classified" replaces the entry (paragraph 17.b).

## 4. What goes to the RS but not into section C
16. Were you presented any personal decoration, Certificate of Commendation, scholarship certificate, or honor or distinguished graduate certificate during the period, and on what date? These are commendatory material under chapter 4 paragraph 4.f(1); the RS handles them in item 6a and section I. They are listed for the RS and are never written as an accomplishment (paragraph 6.c(1)(c)).
17. What PME did you complete this period, resident or nonresident, with the completion date? Chapter 1 paragraph 6.b names PME as something the summary should highlight for the RS.
18. Any pending award recommendation? It is not listed anywhere; the RS "cannot discuss pending award recommendations" (paragraph 4.f(1)(a) Note).
19. For gunnery sergeants: your promotion preference, first sergeant or master sergeant, which the RS records in item 8h from the MROW (paragraph 4.f(8)(a)).

## 5. What is refused
20. Anything adverse or disciplinary about yourself or anyone else, any pending investigation, NJP, or administrative action, any medical, family, or financial circumstance: none of it goes in the MROW. Chapter 4 paragraph 6.c(1)(c) keeps adverse material and disciplinary action out of section C; paragraph 13.d lists what the RS may not comment on at all. If it affected performance, tell the RS in person.
21. Anything about how the report should be marked, where you should rank, or whether you should be promoted. The RS marks sections D through H and writes section I; the RO reviews. The MROW carries facts.

## 6. The reader
22. How does this RS want the MROW written (bullets in past tense, result first, one line each, the order of duties matching the counseling sheet)? Record it in `Overrides/mrow-input.md`.

## The quantification ladder
| Rung | Question |
|---|---|
| Action | What did you do? |
| Scope | For how many, how often, how long, how much? |
| Result | What changed, in a number? |
| Comparison | Against what: the standard the RS set, the unit's prior result, the other sections? |
| Consequence | What did the unit get? |
Stop at the rung the RS could verify. The comparison rung is where an accomplishment turns into a valuation; paragraph 6.c(3)(b) says "avoid creating any unintended valuation", so a comparison names a measured baseline (last year's number, the T&R standard), never another Marine.
