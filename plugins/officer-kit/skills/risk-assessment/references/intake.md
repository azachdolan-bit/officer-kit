# Risk assessment intake

The order's first step is identify hazards, and the sheets that fail are the ones that found three generic hazards instead of the eight real ones. Ask by phase, one phase at a time.

## 1. Frame
1. What is the event, on what dates, where, for how many, with what (weapons, vehicles, water, heights, aircraft, heat or cold)?
2. Who is the OIC and the RSO, by billet? Does the command have its own worksheet form and matrix? (Put them in the override file.)
3. When was this last run, and what went wrong or nearly went wrong? (The best hazard list is last time's near misses.)

## 2. Hazards by phase
For each phase (movement to, setup, each serial of execution, night if any, recovery, movement back), ask:
4. What can hurt someone in this phase? Name the mechanism, not the category: "vehicle rollover on the unimproved road at the 3 km mark in the dark," not "vehicle accident."
5. What can break the event: equipment, weather, a range control call, a missing corpsman?
6. What is different this time from last time (new Marines, new equipment, a different range, a different season)?

## 3. Assess
For each hazard:
7. If it happens, what is the worst credible outcome? That is the severity (I to IV, standard.md). One line why.
8. How often does it happen in events like this, to a unit like this? That is the probability (A to E). One line why. The unit's own history beats a guess.

## 4. Controls
For each hazard:
9. What control changes the probability? (Rehearsal, a speed limit enforced by a lead vehicle, a flag condition check with a work rest cycle, a licensed operator requirement, a second safety at the line.) Who implements it and when?
10. What control changes the severity? (A different distance, a barrier, PPE, a medical asset closer.) Rare; state it when it is real.
11. After the control, what is the probability now, and why? The user says the reason aloud; the sheet records it.

## 5. The required elements (when any residual is IA, IB, IIA, IIB)
12. Emergency action plan: the nearest medical, the evacuation route and time, who calls whom on what net.
13. Cease training and training time out: the word, the signal, who can call it (anyone), what happens next.
14. Communications: primary net, secondary means, check times.
15. Pre execution checklist: what is confirmed the morning of, including anything that changed since signature.

## 6. Supervision
16. For each control, who watches it during the event, and what is the trigger to stop?

## What is not a control
Brief, remind, ensure, emphasize, be aware, stress the importance of. These describe an intention. A control is a thing someone does at a time that changes how often the hazard happens or how bad it is.
